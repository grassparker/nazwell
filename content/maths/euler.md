# Euler's Identity

$$e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$$

This is often called **the most beautiful equation in mathematics.**

It contains five fundamental constants, each from a different branch of mathematics:

| Constant | Value | What It Is |
|----------|-------|------------|
| $e$ | 2.71828... | The base of natural logarithms (Analysis) |
| $i$ | $\sqrt{-1}$ | The imaginary unit (Complex Algebra) |
| $\pi$ | 3.14159... | The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter (Geometry) |
| $1$ | 1 | The multiplicative identity (Arithmetic) |
| $0$ | 0 | The additive identity (Arithmetic) |

All five constants, connected by one simple equation. And it equals zero.

---

## Why It Works

Euler's identity is a special case of Euler's formula:

$$e^{ix} = \cos(x) + i\sin(x)$$

If we plug in $x = \pi$:

$$e^{i\pi} = \cos(\pi) + i\sin(\pi)$$

Since $\cos(\pi) = -1$ and $\sin(\pi) = 0$:

$$e^{i\pi} = -1 + 0i$$

$$e^{i\pi} = -1$$

$$e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$$

## Proof (Taylor Series)

If you want to prove it from scratch:

**Taylor series for $e^{ix}$:**

$$e^{ix} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(ix)^n}{n!}$$

**Taylor series for $\cos(x)$ and $\sin(x)$:**

$$\cos(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n}}{(2n)!}$$

$$\sin(x) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^n x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}$$

If you expand $e^{ix}$ and group the real and imaginary terms, you get:

$$e^{ix} = \cos(x) + i\sin(x)$$

Plug in $x = \pi$:

$$e^{i\pi} = -1$$

$$e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0$$

$$\blacksquare$$
